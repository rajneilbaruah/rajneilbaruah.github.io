About Me

His PhD project “A ‘Deep’ Analysis Framework for Physics Beyond the Standard Model” (DST-CRG/2022/003208):contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} reflects his focus on using deep learning and advanced statistical methods to search for new physics beyond the Standard Model. Rajneil’s work bridges theory and data, applying cutting-edge machine learning (ML) techniques to accelerate inference in high-energy physics and reveal hidden structure in complex experimental data.

With a strong background in theoretical physics (M.Sc. 2020, University of Delhi):contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3} and expertise in computational methods, Rajneil has developed new approaches in simulation-based inference and generative modeling:contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}:contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}. Research

Rajneil’s research focuses on developing and applying modern machine learning methods to solve key challenges in high-energy physics. He works on simulation-based inference, where complex models can be simulated but likelihoods are intractable:contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}. He has investigated hybrid approaches that combine neural networks (e.g. normalizing flows) with traditional samplers like Nested Sampling to accelerate the exploration of multi-modal parameter spaces:contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}:contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}. This enables rapid inference of model parameters in beyond-Standard-Model scenarios, such as studies of neutrino mass models (Type-II Seesaw).

  • Probabilistic Inference and Normalizing Flows: Developing simulation-based inference tools by combining machine-learning models (like normalizing flows) with sampling algorithms to efficiently estimate multi-modal posteriors:contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}. This approach has shown promise for constraining complex BSM parameter spaces more rapidly than traditional methods.
  • Deep Learning for Particle Identification: Applying graph neural networks and convolutional neural networks to classify particle collision data. For example, Rajneil built a GNN-based top-quark tagger to improve signal identification in searches for light supersymmetric Higgsinos:contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}. He also explores lightweight CNN architectures for jet tagging, balancing performance and computational efficiency:contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}.
  • Generative and Diffusion Models: Exploring generative algorithms (VAEs, GANs, diffusion models) for physics applications. This includes using diffusion models for difficult inference problems:contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}, and designing physics-informed generative networks that respect symmetries and conservation laws. These models can serve as fast surrogate simulators or as tools for anomaly detection in large datasets:contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}.
  • Physics-Informed Computing: Incorporating domain knowledge into ML architectures, such as enforcing physical symmetries in deep networks. This work aims to create interpretable, efficient models (e.g. Kolmogorov–Arnold networks) and to build a unified framework for global fits of BSM theories using all available experimental constraints:contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}:contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}.
